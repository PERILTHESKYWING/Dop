# Reference f2: "What is happening on YouTube" (1:15.8, 1920x1080)

XIAO's library file f2, tags studio + do. XIAO's note: smooth, clean movements, a focus point in every scene, holds attention, a beautiful female character (very important). Numbers below come from the code timeline of `lib_get`; the descriptions come from the stills. Sound was not watched (`lib_watch` not run), so voice and music claims are limited to loudness and hit data.

## Structure (a vlog monologue, subtitle-narrated)
Hook: "Guys!" over a close-up of the character at 0:00. Within 4 s she is shocked ("2k subscribers???"), so the stakes are set before any setup. Then a chain of small anecdotes (posture joke, side quest, fujoshi joke, comments, crashout, cooking), each one a self-contained gag, then a CTA ("stay tuned", thanks, "see you in the next video! Bye!") that returns to the same character pose family as the start.

## Pacing (measured)
- 30 shots in 75.8 s: average 2.5 s, median about 2.4 s.
- Most shots 1.2-4.2 s. One long hold of 7.8 s (0:39-0:47, the cheering pose, a line of speech) and 5.4-5.7 s holds on talking lines. Never a dead shot: long holds carry a lot of speech or a small loop.
- Several 0.3-0.6 s "blink" shots (0:01.1, 0:11.3, 0:26.3, 0:53.5) that are really the same pose with a new subtitle or a tiny change; they read as a cut but not a scene change.
- 12 of 29 cuts land within 0.1 s of a sound hit. Loudness stays 3-7 on a 0-9 scale with no silences; no steady beat (talk-driven, not music-driven).
- Cut rate speeds up at the emotional peaks (crashout 0:50-0:54: 1.5 s, 2.1 s, 0.6 s) and relaxes on the outro.

## Camera and framing
- One fixed 16:9 frame, no camera moves. "Camera" is the choice of crop of the same character: head-and-shoulders close-up (most shots), waist-up (intro and outro, arms visible), extreme close-up (crashout: face fills the frame, hands over nose), tiny bust beside a prop (posture gag), full object cut-away (shrimp "you before / you after").
- Every shot has exactly one focus point, usually at the face, placed in the middle-left to centre. The second element (a label, a prop, a meme card) sits on the opposite side and is small and secondary: "SHOCK" scribble right of her face, a 1000 Follower sign above with her hair peeking in at the bottom ("Just woke up"), "Gender" dropdown left with her right.
- Scale changes on every new beat: close, then wide, then close. The same pose is rarely used in two neighbouring beats.
- Subtitle sits at the top centre, white text with black outline, large (about 6% of frame height), one line, replaced per phrase. Faces never sit under it.

## Character design (the "beautiful female character")
- Short bob/messy medium hair, dark maroon at the top fading to raspberry-pink at the tips (gradient hair), loose strands over the forehead and one side lock in front of the ear. Hair is the main shape and is drawn with many separate overlapping locks, not one blob.
- Large red/rose irises with a white highlight and a thin dark upper lash line, slightly asymmetric brows, a small nose mark, blush marks on cheeks. Face is soft and round with a tiny mouth that changes shape per emotion (open shout, ":3" cat mouth, grin with teeth, smirk, tongue-out).
- Skin pale pinkish cream (#f7ddd6 area), a dark brown/maroon outline (not black) on everything, the outfit a dark maroon sleeveless high-neck top, bare shoulders. Outline colour is shared with the text colour of the scribbled labels so the frame feels like one pen.
- Flat colours with one soft hair shading gradient; a cel-shaded polish version only for the crashout close-up (shadow shapes on skin and hair, beige/mauve background) to make the "serious" moment look different.
- Chibi substitutes for secondary gags: a white ghost-like blob with a pencil, a red apple mascot, a shrimp. Characters stay on-model in every version.

## Motion style
- Mostly held cels with small loops: eye blinks, hair sway, a hand wiggle, a breathing bob. The listed motion peaks are low (0-3) for most of the film, with spikes (up to 9) only at shock, the hair-blown wind gag, and the crashout.
- Smear/wind lines over the face (0:16.4), a flat full-frame colour flash (0:16.3, 0.3 s) as a transition, a head drop (0:51.5), a raised arm with a waving hand (0:39 and 1:03). Gestures are exaggerated and read in a thumbnail.
- Doodled handwriting labels ("SHOCK", "My posture", "Just woke up", "Footage of last night", arrows and spiral scribbles) pop in with the beat they describe.
- Sound hits (129 in 75 s) mean about two per second: voice syllables plus SFX; cuts snap to the nearest one.

## Backgrounds
- Default: flat pale sky blue #e4f5ff (a single flat colour, no gradient). The same colour returns after each gag, so colour acts as the "home" state.
- Break colours are used to mark mood change: maroon flash, beige/mauve for the crashout, dusk grey-violet #746b85 for "last night" footage, a grey greyscale doodle kitchen for cooking, a pastel meme card on the right.
- Props are drawn in the same pen: a drawing tablet, desk line, phone, a message bubble.

## What made it work (distilled)
1. Hook in 1 s with a direct address and a face; shock payoff by 4 s.
2. One face, one emotion, one focus point per shot; second element is small and opposite.
3. Each beat is a new crop/pose/prop so no two neighbouring shots look alike, even with one character.
4. Cut rhythm 1.2-4 s, with a few sub-1 s blinks and one long hold for a big line.
5. A recurring "home" background and a sparing break colour for contrast.
6. Hand-written labels and scribbles instead of narration for the joke, which keeps the viewer's eye on the picture.
7. A consistent single outline colour, one hair gradient, and a high design polish on the character (eyes, lashes, hair locks), which is the reason it reads as pretty.
8. Bookend: the intro and outro use the same outfit/crop family, ending on a waving or cute pose and a "Bye!".
